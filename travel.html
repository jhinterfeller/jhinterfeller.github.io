The Place Nobody Put on the Itinerary

The best travel stories usually begin with a reason to stop. Sometimes it is a ruin. Sometimes it is a road that keeps going. Sometimes it is simply the feeling that there must be something over the next hill.

This is where your story goes. Start with the moment you arrived. What did you expect? What did you actually see? Who was there? What surprised you? What detail do you still remember years later?

Don't write a tourism brochure. Write the experience. Let the reader hear the road, smell the place, get rained on with you, get lost with you, and understand why you remember it.

The useful part

After the story, give the reader what they need if they want to follow the trail: how to get there, when you went, what it cost, what you would do differently, and whether you would go back.
